01 / THE BEGINNING
A WINDOW OF OPPORTUNITY

Two probes.
A rare alignment.

In 1977, NASA sent two robotic explorers toward the outer planets. Their journeys were shaped by a rare arrangement of the giant planets — an opportunity to use gravity itself as part of the mission.

AUG 20, 1977

Voyager 2 lifts off

Voyager 2 launched first, beginning a route that would eventually take it past all four giant planets.

SEP 05, 1977

Voyager 1 follows

Voyager 1 launched on a faster trajectory, reaching Jupiter and Saturn before its twin.

1979—1981

Jupiter and Saturn

The spacecraft returned close-up observations that transformed the scientific picture of the outer solar system.

1986—1989

Worlds beyond Saturn

Voyager 2 became the first and, so far, only spacecraft to visit Uranus and Neptune up close.

1990—TODAY

A new kind of journey

After the planetary encounters, both spacecraft continued outward. Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 crossed into interstellar space in 2012 and 2018 respectively.

03 / THE 175-YEAR OPPORTUNITY
A JOURNEY WRITTEN IN ORBITS

Let gravity
do the work.

The outer planets' positions offered a rare opportunity for a sequence of gravity assists. A spacecraft can pass a moving planet and exchange a little energy with it, changing its own speed and direction around the Sun.

ARCHIVAL MEDIA / NASA

See the mission through NASA's archive.

Rather than use a made-up spacecraft animation, this project points to NASA's original mission footage, photographs and engineering imagery. Those records show the spacecraft, its construction and the planetary encounters as they were documented.

04 / THE FAMILY PORTRAIT
FEBRUARY 14, 1990

A world, smaller
than a pixel.

From billions of miles away, Voyager 1 turned its camera back toward home. Earth appeared as a tiny point of light, caught in a scattered ray of sunlight.

PIA23645 / NASA–JPL-CALTECH
“A tiny point of light — and everything we have ever known.”

Voyager 1 captured the image on February 14, 1990, from about 3.7 billion miles (6 billion kilometres) from the Sun. The photograph became part of the first family portrait of the solar system.

The cameras were then turned off to conserve power. The spacecraft's planetary picture-taking chapter was over, but its journey was not.

05 / A MESSAGE FROM EARTH
THE GOLDEN RECORD

A time capsule
for the cosmos.

Each Voyager carries a gold-plated copper phonograph record — a carefully selected introduction to Earth, its life and its cultures.

THE ACTUAL VOYAGER GOLDEN RECORD · NASA/JPL-CALTECH

The record includes greetings in 55 languages, sounds from nature, music from different cultures and eras, and 115 images. Its protective cover carries symbolic instructions intended to explain how to play it and where the spacecraft came from.

06 / BEYOND THE HELIOSPHERE
THE INTERSTELLAR MISSION

The planets were
only the beginning.

After their planetary encounters, the Voyagers continued outward. Both crossed the heliopause — the boundary where the Sun's influence gives way to interstellar space — and began a new chapter of exploration.

VOYAGER 1 / AUGUST 2012

First into interstellar space

Voyager 1 crossed the heliopause in August 2012, becoming the first spacecraft to enter interstellar space.

VOYAGER 2 / NOVEMBER 2018

A second perspective

Voyager 2 crossed the heliopause on November 5, 2018, providing a second set of measurements from a different direction.

The spacecraft use radioisotope power systems. Their available electrical power gradually decreases, so engineers carefully manage the remaining energy and have switched off selected instruments over time. Mission status changes; consult NASA's current mission page for the latest updates.

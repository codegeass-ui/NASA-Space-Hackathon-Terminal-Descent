# 🌌 Echoes in the Dark: The Untold Story of Voyager 1 & 2

**An interactive educational storytelling project exploring the legacy of NASA's Voyager missions and the spacecraft that continue to carry humanity's discoveries into deep space.**

Developed for the **2026 NASA International Space Apps Challenge** under the challenge *Abandoned but not Forgotten: Storytelling about NASA's Discarded Equipment on the Moon and Mars and Deep Space.*

**Team:** Terminal Descent  
**Creator:** Hrinika Sharma

---

##  Project Overview

What happens to spacecraft when their missions begin to end? Do their stories end when their instruments go silent?

*Echoes in the Dark* explores the extraordinary journey of NASA's twin spacecraft, Voyager 1 and Voyager 2, launched in 1977 to explore the outer Solar System.

The project transforms decades of space exploration history into an accessible educational experience for school-age learners and space enthusiasts. Through an interactive website and a short educational film, it follows the Voyager missions from their historic planetary encounters to their journey into interstellar space.

Rather than presenting retired spacecraft as forgotten pieces of technology, the project highlights their scientific contributions, engineering achievements, and lasting connection to humanity.

## The Challenge

**NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026**

*Abandoned but not Forgotten: Storytelling about NASA's Discarded Equipment on the Moon and Mars and Deep Space.*

The challenge focused on introducing school-age space enthusiasts to the history of NASA's spacecraft, landers, rovers, and scientific instruments, and explaining the discoveries these machines made possible.

Our approach was to tell the story of Voyager 1 and 2 through visual storytelling, combining scientific research, interactive web elements, and educational filmmaking.

##  Key Features

### 1. Interactive Educational Website

The website serves as a visual companion to the Voyager story, featuring:

- **Deep-Space Visual Experience:** Animated starfield, atmospheric backgrounds, and responsive layouts.
- **The 175-Year Planetary Alignment:** An introduction to the rare planetary geometry that made the Voyager Grand Tour possible.
- **Spacecraft Engineering:** Exploration of radioisotope thermoelectric generators (RTGs), nuclear power systems, and deep-space communication.
- **Interactive Power Timeline:** A visual representation of the gradual decline in Voyager's electrical power and the retirement of onboard instruments.
- **The Grand Tour:** Planetary discoveries made during encounters with Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.
- **The Pale Blue Dot:** The story behind Voyager 1's iconic 1990 photograph of Earth.
- **The Golden Record Explorer:** Interactive sections explaining the sounds, languages, photographs, and scientific information carried aboard both spacecraft.

### 2. Educational Documentary-Style Video

The project includes a short educational film that takes viewers through the Voyager missions using:

- Historical NASA mission footage and archival imagery.
- Planetary flyby discoveries.
- Custom orbital alignment animation.
- Narration designed for younger audiences.
- Background music and visual storytelling.

The film connects Voyager's fading power and retiring instruments with the broader story of NASA's legacy hardware on the Moon and Mars.

### 3. Project Presentation

A supporting presentation documents the project's concept, challenge alignment, research methodology, storytelling approach, and production workflow.

## 🪐 Inside the Voyager Journey

The educational film follows four major chapters:

| Chapter | Focus |
|---|---|
| Act I: The 175-Year Alignment | The 1977 launches, spacecraft engineering, and planetary alignment. |
| Act II: The Grand Tour | Discoveries at Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. |
| Act III: The Pale Blue Dot | Voyager 1's final photograph of Earth in 1990. |
| Act IV: The Golden Record | Humanity's message carried aboard both spacecraft. |

## Research & Development Process

The project was developed through four main stages:

**1. Research and Fact-Checking**

Collected Voyager mission timelines, launch information, planetary encounters, spacecraft specifications, RTG power data, and historical discoveries using NASA and JPL resources.

**2. Scriptwriting and Story Development**

Translated technical concepts such as gravity-assist trajectories, nuclear power decay, and instrument shutdowns into simpler explanations suitable for younger audiences.

**3. Website and Video Development**

Created an interactive website using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Produced the educational film by combining archival footage, custom animations, narration, and music.

**4. Presentation and Publishing**

Prepared a project demonstration explaining the concept, research, and production workflow. Published the educational film on YouTube to make it accessible to students, teachers, and space enthusiasts.

## Educational Impact

Space exploration is often presented through the excitement of new launches and upcoming missions. However, the machines that made earlier discoveries possible deserve attention too.

This project aims to:

- Make NASA's historical mission archives more accessible to younger audiences.
- Introduce students to spacecraft engineering and planetary science.
- Demonstrate how scientific discoveries are built across generations of missions.
- Encourage curiosity about space exploration and the technology behind it.
- Preserve the stories of spacecraft whose scientific journeys are gradually coming to an end.

The central idea is simple: **A spacecraft's mission may end, but its contribution to science does not.**
